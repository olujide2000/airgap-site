Airgap turns your recordings into text on your own Windows PC. The audio is never uploaded, never stored on someone else's server, and never seen by anyone but you. Unplug the network cable and it still works.

Every cloud transcription service — Rev, Otter, Descript, Sonix — works by uploading your recording to their servers. For a lot of professional work, that single step is the problem. Airgap removes it.

Who this is for

Lawyers and paralegals Client interviews, depositions, hearing recordings. Uploading privileged material to a third-party service can itself be the breach.
Journalists Source protection. A cloud provider is a third party that can be subpoenaed, breached, or served a warrant you never hear about.
Therapists and clinicians Session recordings and dictation are special-category data under HIPAA and GDPR. Most cloud tools need a signed agreement you may not have.
Academic researchers Ethics approval commonly forbids processing interview data on third-party services. Local processing sidesteps the whole question.
Podcasters and agencies Fifty files at once with no per-minute bill and no upload wait.

What it does

  • Drop in files or whole folders. Queue them up and walk away — it works through them and can run overnight.
  • Exports the formats professionals actually file: Word (.docx), plain text, CSV, plus SRT and VTT subtitles.
  • Click any line to jump to that moment in the audio, and correct it inline. Find-and-replace across the whole transcript.
  • Runs completely offline once installed. No account, no sign-in, no telemetry, no licence server phoning home.
  • Speaker labels — who said what — and custom vocabulary for names and technical terms.
