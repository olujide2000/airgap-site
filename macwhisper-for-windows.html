MacWhisper for Windows

Short answer: it doesn't exist, and it isn't coming. Here's the actual reason, and an honest look at what Windows users can use instead — including options that aren't mine.

Why there's no Windows version

MacWhisper is a genuinely good app from an independent Mac developer. It runs OpenAI's Whisper model locally so your audio never leaves your machine, and it sells for a one-time fee rather than a subscription. It has passed 300,000 downloads.

But it's built on parts of macOS that have no Windows equivalent — Apple Silicon acceleration for the local models, and more recently Apple's own on-device speech engine. Those aren't features that can be flagged on for Windows; they're the foundation the app is built on. A Windows version wouldn't be a port. It would be a rewrite.

That's why searching for "MacWhisper for Windows" turns up nothing official, and probably always will.

What Windows users actually have

A fair summary of the landscape in 2026, including the free options:

ToolCostWhat it's really for
Subtitle EditFree, open source Subtitle timing and syncing. Powerful and mature. Can run Whisper, but you choose engines, download models and point it at binaries yourself.
Whisper dictation toolsFree to ~$29 Live dictation — speaking and having text appear as you talk. A different job from transcribing recordings you already have.
Cloud servicesPer minute or monthly Rev, Otter, Sonix, Descript. Fast and collaborative — but they work by uploading your recording to their servers.
Airgap (in development)One-time Batch transcription of existing recordings, offline, with professional export formats. Built for people who can't upload.

Honestly: which should you use?

If you subtitle video, use Subtitle Edit. It's free, it's excellent at timing and syncing, and it does that job better than anything I'm going to build. I'd rather tell you that than pretend otherwise.

If you want to talk and see text appear, you want a dictation tool. Several are free and good.

If you have recordings you cannot upload — client interviews, patient sessions, research data, confidential source material — and you need clean transcripts in Word or CSV rather than subtitle files, that's the gap Airgap is being built for.

The distinction that matters: Subtitle Edit is built for a subtitler. Airgap is built for a professional with confidential audio who doesn't care about video at all, and shouldn't have to learn about model files to get a transcript.
